Is Math Biased?

AI vs Human Error Analyser · comparing AI predictions, human predictions and the correct answers
MATH+AI FUTURES · OUR BIG QUESTION

Is math biased? Can the formula we choose decide whether AI or humans come out on top?

People say an AI is "more accurate" than humans as if accuracy were one number. It isn't: every error formula notices some mistakes and ignores others. This site lets you test whether the choice of formula can change who looks like the winner.

The problem

AI systems are judged by numbers like "95% accurate" or "error of 2.3", and those numbers decide whether people trust an AI over a person. But the number depends on which formula was used. If the formula hides the kind of mistake that matters, a harmful AI can look excellent on paper.

The mathematics we use

Every measure here starts from one idea: error = prediction − correct answer.

  • Mean error — shows direction, but positive and negative errors cancel.
  • Absolute value (MAE) — removes the sign so errors cannot cancel.
  • Squares and square roots (RMSE) — make big misses count much more.
  • Median and maximum — the typical miss versus the worst miss.

The answer in 30 seconds

The correct answers are 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60. The AI is perfect five times and misses once by 10. The humans are off by exactly 2 every time.

Same data, opposite verdicts. Every number is calculated correctly. The bias is not in the arithmetic; it is in choosing which formula to report.

How this connects to AI

AI models are trained by making one chosen error formula as small as possible, and tested with the same formulas before they are used. So the formula shapes what an AI learns to care about, and decides whether it is called "good enough".

Two meanings of "bias" on this site

Statistical bias is a number: the mean error. It shows whether predictions lean too high or too low.

Bias in the judging is our Big Question: a formula that quietly favours one side or hides one kind of mistake. A formula can show zero statistical bias and still be a biased way to judge.
